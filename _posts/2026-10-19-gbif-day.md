As the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) celebrates its 25th anniversary on the 19th October 2026, We take this occasion to reflect on a quarter-century of transformative progress in open biodiversity data. Since its inception in 2001, GBIF has built the essential cyberinfrastructure for modern macroecology and conservation, mobilizing billions of occurrence records worldwide. A prime example of how this global framework drives regional scientific discovery and high-level policy is the SCAR (Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research) Antarctic Biodiversity Portal (biodiversity.aq). The enduring partnership between GBIF and the biodiversity.aq portal illustrates a highly successful, bidirectional flow of data and technology, functioning as a vital bridge between isolated polar observations and international environmental research and governance.

The SCAR Antarctic Biodiversity Portal serves as the premier data hub for the Antarctic and Southern Ocean, relying heavily on the informatics architecture pioneered by GBIF to manage complex datasets from the planet's most remote environments. Rather than building web architecture from scratch, biodiversity.aq currently utilizes GBIF’s hosted portal services to maintain a robust, searchable front-end.

> "GBIF’s infrastructure, particularly the Integrated Publishing Toolkit (IPT) and their hosted portal services, has been a game-changer," explains [Yi-Ming Gan](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-7087-2646), Data Manager for the SCAR Antarctic Biodiversity Portal. "It reduces maintenance burden and allows us to focus our energy on what we do best: curating complex polar datasets, standardizing data in FAIR manner, and ensuring taxonomic rigor while relying on GBIF's infrastructure to make this data universally accessible."

While SCAR leverages these global tools, biodiversity.aq is equally a key cornerstone to GBIF’s mission of representing all life on Earth. The Antarctic and Southern Ocean represent historically difficult regions to study, leading to significant geographic data gaps in the global index. By aggregating more than two million marine and terrestrial records from specialized regional networks, the SCAR portal directly addresses this deficit. Furthermore, through the Register of Antarctic Species (RAMS), biodiversity.aq curates a rigorously peer-reviewed taxonomic baseline of Antarctic organisms.

> "In return for the technological support, we feed high-quality, expert-validated data from one of the world's most inaccessible regions back into the global GBIF index, enriching the universal taxonomic backbone for poorly studied polar taxa," Gan notes.

This robust data pipeline is not just an academic exercise; it is the fundamental evidence base for international environmental management. Antarctica is governed by consensus through the Antarctic Treaty System (ATS), making transparent, objective, and accessible scientific data a strict prerequisite for policy decisions.

> “The data mobilized through this partnership directly informs Ecosystem based management  through the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR).” explains [Pablo Deschepper](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-6088-1290), biodiversity data specialist at the SCAR Antarctic Biodiversity Portal. “By developing tools and data products such as [SOfish](https://www.biodiversity.aq/sofish/), the biodiversity.aq team aims to bring biodiversity data to life” he adds.

Anton Van de Putte, Science Officer for SCAR and a driving force behind biodiversity.aq, highlights the critical policy translation of their work.

> "In the context of the Antarctic Treaty System, objective and open data is the currency of consensus," [Anton Van de Putte](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-1336-5554) states. "By mobilizing historical baselines and contemporary observations through GBIF, we provide bodies like CCAMLR and the Committee for Environmental Protection with the precise evidence base they need. Whether it is designating Marine Protected Areas, tracking climate-driven range shifts along the rapidly warming Antarctic Peninsula, or implementing biosecurity protocols to detect non-native species, the data must be readily available to decision-makers."

As GBIF marks 25 years of democratizing biodiversity data, its symbiosis with regional nodes like the SCAR Antarctic Biodiversity Portal demonstrates the true value of open science. By transforming fragmented field observations into FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) data, this partnership not only advances our fundamental understanding of extreme ecosystems but provides the critical foresight required to protect them in an era of rapid global change.
